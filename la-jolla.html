A coastal community inside the City of San Diego, with ocean view homes, a walkable Village and some of the county's best known beaches and parks.

Living in La Jolla

La Jolla is a community of the City of San Diego, and it is made up of several distinct pockets. The Village around Prospect Street and Girard Avenue is the walkable center, Bird Rock has a small beach town feel, La Jolla Shores is a flat, gentle beach stretch, Windansea is known for its surf, and the hills of Muirlands and La Jolla Farms sit above the ocean.

Its landmarks include La Jolla Cove, the Children's Pool with its harbor seals, Scripps Institution of Oceanography, UC San Diego, the Birch Aquarium, Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve and the city's Torrey Pines golf courses. Mount Soledad, with the Mt. Soledad National Veterans Memorial, gives wide views over the coast.

Homes you'll find

  • Ocean view and bluff top homes: some on narrow streets with views of the Pacific or La Jolla Cove.
  • Village condos and townhomes: within walking distance of shops, restaurants and the water.
  • Beach cottages and mid-century homes: in Bird Rock, Windansea and the streets near the coast.
  • Estates in Muirlands, La Jolla Farms and the hills: on larger lots with views.
  • Villas and gated communities: with HOA dues and shared amenities.

Getting around and schools

La Jolla is served by San Diego Unified School District, with La Jolla Elementary, Bird Rock Elementary and La Jolla High among the neighborhood schools, and several private schools also operate in the area. I can help you confirm the assigned schools for any address.

I-5 and SR-52 are the main freeways, and La Jolla Village Drive connects the area to UC San Diego and the Golden Triangle. The Village is busiest on summer weekends, and locals know the easy times and routes to get around.

Buying in La Jolla

La Jolla is one of the most beautiful places to live in the world: the Cove, the Shores, world class dining, UC San Diego and the Scripps Institution nearby, and views that never get old. Homes here have a long history of strong demand.

Most of La Jolla sits in the California coastal zone, and the city’s voter approved 30 foot coastal height limit applies west of I-5, which helps protect the views everyone loves. If you are planning a remodel, we will look early at what is possible on the lot. For bluff and slope properties, we will review the geotechnical reports and get insurance quotes up front, and for older homes we will check whether a historic review could apply. With that homework done, you can buy with complete confidence.

Selling in La Jolla

La Jolla buyers focus on the exact block, the view and the walk to the water. We will show the view in great light and place your home within its pocket of La Jolla, not just the name.

Buyers at this level appreciate a complete file, so having permits, geotechnical and drainage reports, and any historic documentation ready makes your home stand out.

La Jolla FAQ

Is La Jolla a separate city?

No. La Jolla is a community within the City of San Diego.

What are the main parts of La Jolla?

The Village, Bird Rock, La Jolla Shores, Windansea, Muirlands and La Jolla Farms are among the best known.

What should I know about remodeling in La Jolla?

Most of La Jolla is in the California coastal zone, and the city’s 30 foot coastal height limit applies west of I-5, which helps protect views. Older homes can also involve a historic review. I help buyers understand what is possible on a lot before they buy.

Which schools serve La Jolla?

San Diego Unified serves the area, including La Jolla Elementary, Bird Rock Elementary and La Jolla High, and private schools operate nearby.
